01

Context & concept

Context

Blaines' previous project, Wherever You Like: 12 songs released over 12 months, each with visuals shot in a different country.

Concept

Those trips had a hidden purpose. In every country, Blaines was wiring a network across the world. Now he hijacks it, flooding the world's transmissions with music and pure expression, at a relentless rate.

Project length: ongoing.

  1. STEP 1
    The introduction skit
    A short film that connects the previous era to this one and explains the concept.
  2. STEP 2
    A new demo every day
    Each one paired with short-form clips in a digital, hacker aesthetic.
  3. STEP 3
    Exclusive on Untitled
    Demos are only available through an email sign-up, "connecting to the network". This builds the core fanbase.
  4. STEP 4
    The best get released
    Each month, the highest-performing demos get a full release on streaming platforms and a music video.

02

The introduction skit

01
The abandoned building

A group of kids outside an abandoned building. Two are doom-scrolling on their phones. The third calls them boring ("Brain rot, smh") and goes inside alone to explore.

02
The discovery

He finds a room at the top of the building and peers round the door. Blaines is levitating in the middle of the room, his arms wired by cables to computers and a transmitter.

03
Wiring the network

Cut to twelve months earlier. Quick clips of Blaines in different countries setting up the system: cell towers, electrical boxes, antennas and satellite dishes.

Higgsfield AI is used selectively on these short clips, for example to replace backgrounds. No full scene generation.

04
Connection ready

Back in the UK. Blaines checks every country is connected on screen, then hooks the cables up to himself.

05
Welcome to the network

A hard switch into a short 2D explainer video, inspired by the style of DedSec's broadcasts in the game Watch Dogs. It lays out the state of the world: our attention is being farmed and sold. Consumption now far outweighs expression, and the result is weaker art, fewer legends, and fewer people reaching real brilliance. Then: "Welcome to the network."

Signals · Daily demos

Every demo is a signal, posted as a transmission to the network. Demos will also be released in long form, in vertical and horizontal versions.

The hook

Posts open on the hook of a highly viral video, which is then interrupted by Blaines' post. The interruption looks like a system warning, as if the viewer's phone has been hijacked.
